Notes

What the rating rests on
  1. Measurement coverage is 51%. This is how much of the full 7-category skill picture we can measure from one camera yet — not how sure we are of your rating. It's low mainly because shot quality (pace, dink height, return depth) isn't measured yet; the counts we do report are validated. Thresholds are uncalibrated heuristics anchored to the USAPA definitions, not an official rating.
Where the level descriptions come from
  1. Level descriptions are a condensed synthesis of the published USA Pickleball definitions across the seven categories.
How positioning and movement are measured
  1. Positioning is measured from the player's front foot, during live rallies only (between-point standing is excluded).
  2. Court covered is a work-rate figure (feet per minute of play). On its own it isn't good or bad — strong players often move less but get to better spots — so we don't rate it. The coachable lever is footwork and positioning, which lives under Strategy above.
How shots and bounces are counted
  1. A shot played off the bounce follows exactly one bounce. Serves do not — a serve is struck from the hand — and neither do volleys, taken out of the air. That leaves 272 shots here that should each follow a bounce, against 277 bounces detected, so the two agree. Part of any shortfall is not a missing bounce at all: where we read a volley as a ground shot we expect a bounce that never existed, and on the clips with shot-by-shot review that accounts for about half of it. The rest thins the landing map and depth statistics, but does not affect your positioning or rating.
  2. Every shot count in this report is shots played during rallies. 75 further contacts were detected outside any rally — warm-up hits, balls picked up and returned between points, and some false detections — and are excluded, because they are not shots played in a point. So this is smaller than the raw detection count, on purpose.
